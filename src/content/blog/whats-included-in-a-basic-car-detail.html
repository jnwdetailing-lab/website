What a Basic Detail Usually Covers

A basic detail is a careful clean of the surfaces you see. Most include:

  • A hand wash, rinse and dry
  • Wheels and tires cleaned
  • A vacuum of the seats, carpets, mats and trunk
  • The dash, door panels and console wiped down
  • Windows and mirrors cleaned inside and out

That is a solid tune-up for a car that gets washed regularly and has no stains or smells.

What a Basic Detail Usually Leaves Out

  • Steam cleaning and stain treatment. A basic interior gets vacuumed and wiped, not steamed. Set-in stains need hot-water extraction.
  • Clay bar decontamination. Paint that feels rough after a wash has bonded grit, like brake dust, which bonds to wheels and paint over time. Only a clay bar pulls that out.
  • Polishing. Swirl marks and dullness sit in the clear coat. Washing cleans the paint; only machine polishing levels it.
  • Lasting protection. A basic package may include a quick spray wax at most, not a sealant or coating.
  • Engine bay and headlights. Those are separate jobs everywhere.

Basic or Full? A Quick Check

A lighter clean is probably enough if the paint feels smooth after a wash, there are no stains or smells, and the car was properly detailed in the last few months. Book a full detail if the seats or carpet have stains, a smell keeps coming back, the paint feels rough, or it has been six months or more.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a basic detail and a full detail?

A basic detail cleans the visible surfaces: a wash, a vacuum and a wipe-down. A full detail goes deeper, with steam cleaning, stain treatment, conditioned leather and every jamb and window, inside and out.

Is seat shampooing included in a basic detail?

Usually not. A basic interior gets vacuumed and wiped down. At J&W, steam cleaning and spot stain treatment are part of every interior detail, and a hot-water shampoo for set-in stains is a {{addon:Shampoo seats (stain removal)}} add-on.

Does a basic detail remove swirl marks?

No. Swirls are in the clear coat and only machine polishing removes them. A wash and wax can make them a little less visible for a few weeks, but they come back.
