Is It Worth It for Your Car? A Quick Check

A full detail is a good use of money when two or more of these are true:

  • The paint feels rough when you run your fingertips over it after a wash
  • The seats or carpet have stains, or there is a smell vacuuming won't fix
  • You just bought a used car, or you are getting ready to sell or trade in
  • The headlights look yellow or hazy
  • It has been six months or more since the last real detail

Frequently Asked Questions

Is car detailing worth the money compared with just washing my car?

For most cars, yes, a few times a year. A wash removes loose dirt. A detail cleans the cabin properly, protects the paint and handles the grime a wash leaves behind. Washing between details is still the right habit, because it keeps each detail looking good longer.

Does detailing increase resale value?

It helps the car show at its best. Buyers and appraisers notice clean paint, clear headlights and a fresh interior right away, and a car that has been detailed regularly hides fewer problems like set-in stains or etched paint.

Should I detail my car myself or hire a professional?

Vacuuming and wiping down surfaces are easy to do yourself. Steam cleaning, hot-water extraction, clay bar work and machine polishing take equipment and practice, and done wrong they can scratch paint or leave seats soaked. For anything beyond a quick clean-up, a professional gets a safer, longer-lasting result.
