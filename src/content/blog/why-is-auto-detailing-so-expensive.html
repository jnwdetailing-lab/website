Where the Money Goes

Time is the biggest cost. A proper detail means a careful hand wash, wheels, trim, door jambs and glass outside, and a double vacuum, an air purge, steam cleaning of every surface and stain treatment inside. Done right, that is {{FULL_HOURS}} for one car. There are no shortcuts that keep the quality.

  • Equipment. Commercial steamers, hot-water extractors, polishers, pressure washers and good vacuums are expensive and wear out.
  • Products. pH-balanced soaps, dedicated wheel cleaners, leather conditioners, plastic protectants and a large stock of clean microfiber towels. Cheap products and reused dirty towels are how paint gets scratched.
  • Mobile setup. A mobile detailer carries a water tank and power to your driveway, plus fuel and drive time between jobs.
  • Insurance and licensing. A legitimate business is licensed and insured, which protects you if anything goes wrong. We are both.
  • Skill. Knowing which product is safe on which surface, how hard to push a stain, and when to stop takes years to learn.

Why Size and Condition Change the Price

A bigger or dirtier car takes longer, so it costs more. A three-row SUV has much more carpet, seating and paint than a sedan, which is why our Full Detail is {{FULL_SEDAN}} for a coupe or sedan, {{FULL_SUV5}} for a 5-seat SUV or truck and {{FULL_SUV7}} for a 7-seat SUV or minivan.

Condition matters just as much. Dog hair woven into carpet, sand from a beach trip, years of crumbs in the seat rails, or sap baked onto the paint can each add an hour or more. Sap is a good example: it hardens on warm paint and has to be softened and lifted without scratching the clear coat (AAA has a guide on removing sap safely), which takes time. Why Some Detailers Are So Cheap

A very low price usually means something is missing: fewer hours, a brush instead of a hand wash, the same towel on every car, no steam or stain treatment, or no insurance. Ask any detailer for the full list of steps and how long the job takes. If they can't tell you, the low price is the answer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does a bigger vehicle cost more to detail?

More carpet, more seats, more glass and more paint means more hours. A 7-seat SUV or minivan can take noticeably longer than a sedan, so the price goes up with the size.

Is a detail worth it compared with a car wash?

They do different jobs. A wash removes loose dirt in minutes. A detail is hours of hand work that cleans the cabin properly and protects the paint. Most cars do best with regular washes and a detail a few times a year.

Does mobile detailing cost more than a shop?

Not necessarily. A mobile detailer has no shop rent but does carry water, power and drive time. You save the trip and the wait, and the work happens in your driveway.

Will the price change after the detailer sees my car?

It shouldn't change without your OK. With us, anything beyond normal condition, like heavy pet hair or set-in stains, is a set add-on we agree on before we start.
