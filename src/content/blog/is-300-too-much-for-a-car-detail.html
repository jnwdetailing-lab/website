When $300 Is Too Much

A quote around $300 is not worth it if any of these are true:

  • The "detail" is really a wash, a vacuum and a wipe-down that takes under an hour.
  • Nobody can tell you exactly what is included, or the list changes after you book.
  • The interior gets a vacuum but no steam, stain treatment or conditioning.
  • The paint gets washed with the same dirty mitt or a brush, which is how swirl marks start.
  • The price jumps at the end with surprise charges you never agreed to.

Where $300 Fits Against Bigger Jobs

A full detail cleans and protects. It does not repair paint. Paint correction, ceramic coating and engine bay or headlight work are separate, and they are priced on top. Brake dust is a good example of why: it is made of fine metal particles that bond to wheels and, left long enough, can damage the finish, so heavily caked wheels take more time than a normal clean.

Is $300 a lot for a car detail?

Not for a full interior and exterior detail on a coupe or sedan. It is a lot for a wash and vacuum. Look at the list of steps and the time it takes. Our Full Detail is {{FULL_SEDAN}} for a coupe or sedan and takes {{FULL_HOURS}}.

Why do two cars of the same model get different prices?

Condition. A car with pet hair, sand, stains or a smell takes much longer than the same model that is lightly used. We price those as set add-ons and agree on them before we start, so there are no surprises.

Is mobile detailing less thorough than a shop?

It shouldn't be. A mobile detailer brings the same tools to you: steam, extraction, polishers, plus water and power. What you are paying for is the checklist and the hours, not the building.

Does a $300 detail include ceramic coating?

No. A ceramic coating is a separate, longer job that includes paint prep and is quoted per vehicle. A full detail can add a ceramic sealant upgrade ({{addon:Ceramic sealant upgrade (6 months)}}) that protects for about six months.
