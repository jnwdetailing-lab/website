Quick Answer

A car detailer cleans, restores and protects a vehicle by hand, inside and out, with the tools and products to reach what a wash or a home vacuum can't. Outside that means a careful hand wash, wheels, trim, jambs and glass, then protection. Inside it means vacuuming, air-purging, steam cleaning and stain treatment. Paint correction, coatings and other specialty work are separate services on top of that.

"Detailing" gets used for everything from a quick wash to a full day of polishing, so it is fair to ask what you are actually paying for. This guide walks through what a professional detailer does on each half of the car, the order it happens in, and what is usually an add-on. It is the same breakdown we give customers before every job at J&W, where we do mobile car detailing in Oakland and the surrounding cities.

Outside: Clean, Then Protect

Exterior detailing gets the paint, wheels and glass truly clean without scratching them, then adds a layer of protection. In our Full Detail that is:

  • Pre-soak. A foam that loosens bugs, bird droppings and road grime before anything touches the paint.
  • Professional hand wash. Clean mitts and a two-bucket method, so grit is not dragged across the clear coat.
  • Wheels and tires. Wheel wells pressure-washed, rim faces and tires detailed with dedicated brushes, then tires dressed.
  • Trim, door jambs and exterior windows. The areas a drive-through wash skips.
  • High-gloss spray wax. Shine plus a few weeks of protection, so water beads and dirt rinses off.

Brake dust deserves a mention. It is made of fine metal and carbon particles that bake onto wheels, and left long enough it can pit the finish. Cars that run 580 or 880 every day collect it fast, which is why wheels get their own tools and attention.

Inside: Where Most of the Hours Go

Interior detailing cleans everything you touch and breathe. On a typical car it takes more time than the outside. Our Full Detail interior includes:

  • A double vacuum of the seats, carpets, mats and trunk
  • An air purge that blows dust and crumbs out of the vents, seams, cupholders and crevices
  • Steam cleaning of the seats, carpets, mats, door panels, console and plastics
  • Spot stain treatment
  • Leather cleaned and conditioned, plastics cleaned and protected, both left with a matte finish
  • Windows, mirrors and visors, the trunk detailed, and the headliner spot-cleaned as needed

Steam does a lot of the heavy lifting. It loosens sticky spills in cupholders and door pockets and sanitizes without soaking the upholstery.

Why the Order Matters

Each step sets up the next one, so a good detailer works in a set order. Do it out of sequence and you undo your own work.

  • Dry before wet inside. Vacuum and air purge first. Shampooing a seat that still has sand in it just pushes the dirt deeper.
  • Wheels before paint. Brake dust is heavy and greasy, and scrubbing it splashes. Clean paint should not get splattered after it is washed.
  • Clean before you polish. Bonded grit has to come off before a polishing pad touches the paint, or the pad grinds it in. That is how people add scratches while trying to remove them.
  • Protection goes on last. A wax, sealant or ceramic coating bonds to whatever is on the surface, so it only goes onto paint that is fully clean.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a detailer do that a car wash doesn't?

A detailer works by hand on the whole car. Outside that means a gentle hand wash, wheels, trim, door jambs and protection. Inside it means vacuuming, an air purge, steam cleaning and stain treatment. A car wash only removes loose dirt from the outside.

Does a full detail remove scratches and swirl marks?

No. A full detail makes paint look much better, but swirls and scratches sit in the clear coat and only machine polishing removes them. Light swirls come out with our {{addon:Polish}} polish add-on. Deeper damage needs paint correction.

Our Full Detail takes {{FULL_HOURS}} inside and out. Add-ons and heavy pet hair or buildup add time, and we agree on that before we start.

Will my seats be wet after a detail?

Steam cleaning leaves seats only slightly damp. A hot-water shampoo uses more water, so shampooed fabric can stay damp for a few hours. Cracking the windows helps it dry faster.
