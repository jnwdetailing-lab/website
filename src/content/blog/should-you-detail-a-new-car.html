Quick Answer

A brand-new car rarely needs a deep clean, but it is the best time to prepare and protect the paint. New cars often arrive with transport fallout, adhesive residue and light swirls from a quick dealer wash. Cleaning that off and adding protection while the paint is at its best is easier and cheaper than fixing it a few years from now. If you plan to keep the car, protect it early. If it is a short lease you will return as-is, a lighter approach is fine.

It sounds strange to pay to detail a car with 20 miles on it. But "new" and "perfect" are not the same thing, and the first few weeks are when protection does the most good. Here is what a new car usually needs, what it doesn't, and how to decide. We see a lot of new cars at J&W, where we do mobile car detailing in Oakland and around the East Bay.

Dealer Prep Is Not a Detail

Dealer prep gets a car presentable for delivery. It is not the same as a professional detail. On new cars it is common to find:

  • Fine metal fallout picked up during shipping, which makes the paint feel slightly rough
  • Sticky residue from transport film and window stickers
  • Light swirls or haze from a fast wash with shared towels
  • Tire shine slung onto the lower doors

None of that is a disaster. It is just easier to deal with before anything gets sealed over it.

The Pros of Detailing a New Car

  • The paint will never be in better shape. That makes now the best time for protection: a ceramic sealant ({{addon:Ceramic sealant upgrade (6 months)}}, about six months) or a professional ceramic coating for years.
  • Small flaws are cheap to fix now. A light polish takes out dealer-wash swirls before a coating locks the finish in. Our Bronze ceramic package includes a paint enhancement polish and is built for newer cars with paint in good shape.
  • Easier upkeep. Protected paint and wheels release dirt, sap and brake dust more easily, so every wash after that is faster.
  • A head start on the street. Sap and bird droppings can etch bare clear coat, which Consumer Reports recommends dealing with early. Protection buys you time when the car sits under a tree.

The Cons, Honestly

  • Cost. Protection is money spent on a car that already looks good. It pays off over years, not weeks.
  • Short leases. If you will return the car in two years and won't park it outside, heavy protection may not be worth it. A careful wash routine might be all you need.
  • It is not magic. A coating makes paint easier to clean and more resistant to stains and etching. It does not make the car scratch-proof or maintenance-free. You still wash it.
How to Decide

Ask three questions. How long will you keep the car? Where does it sleep, a garage or the street under trees? How do you wash it? Years of ownership, street parking and a busy schedule all point toward protecting it now. A short lease in a garage points toward a lighter approach.

What About the Interior?

A new interior usually just needs to stay that way. If you have kids, pets or a long commute, an interior detail every few months keeps it from ever getting to the stained stage. Our detailing plans make that easy on a monthly, every-two-months or quarterly schedule.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is dealer prep the same as a full detail?

No. Dealer prep makes a car presentable for delivery. It usually doesn't include decontaminating the paint, removing adhesive residue properly or polishing out wash swirls, and it doesn't add lasting protection.

How can I tell if my new car already has swirls?

Look at the paint in direct sun or under a bright flashlight. Fine circular scratches or a hazy look mean swirls. Run your fingertips over the hood after a wash; if it feels rough, there is bonded fallout on the paint.

Should I get a ceramic coating on a brand-new car?

If you plan to keep it for years, new is the ideal time, because the paint needs the least correction before coating. Every coating is quoted per vehicle after we see the paint.

Is it worth detailing a leased car?

For a short lease, a good wash routine and a detail before you turn it in may be enough. If you park outside or plan to buy it out, protection is more worth it.
